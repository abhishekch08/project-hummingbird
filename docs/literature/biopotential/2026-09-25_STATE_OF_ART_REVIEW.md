## Normalized quantitative comparison

Keep each source's passband, impedance test frequency, circuit boundary, source contact and technology alongside the result. A peak-to-peak datasheet noise figure cannot be converted to rms by a fixed factor without a defined observation window and distribution. E1's 0.12 µV rms/100 Hz, E2's dynamic-range/SNDR, V1's 1 µVpp/70 Hz, V2's 6–10 µVpp/40–150 Hz and S2's simulated 1.04 µV rms/1 kHz are **not equivalent noise results**. E1 uses a chip IA without pads/electrodes/ADC; E2 reports AFE converter power; E3/E4 measure electrodes and motion, not circuit NEF. No Hummingbird area, die yield or actual on-head signal-quality value can be normalized from them.

Illustrative arithmetic **for sensitivity only**: a hypothetical 120 kΩ real source feeding 1 GΩ input gives 0.99988 voltage fraction at DC in a simple divider (ignoring pad capacitance). A 300 pA bias across 120 kΩ adds 36 µV DC; at an assumed 1 MΩ contact it adds 300 µV. A 1 V common-mode signal with ideal 100 dB rejection leaves 10 µV equivalent differential before electrode mismatch and cable pickup. With an uncorrected ±300 mV electrode DC offset, gain ×128 requires 38.4 V output headroom in a simple DC-coupled chain. These numbers are **not** measurements of any listed AFE. They demonstrate that loading, bias/leakage, mismatch, interference and offset need distinct budgets and gain states.

## Architecture families and trade

| Family | Performance/volume/power opportunity | Known failure mechanism and gate action |
|---|---|---|
| DC-coupled per-channel PGA and ΔΣ ADC (V1 pattern) | Preserves very low frequency EEG and offset information; simultaneous channels simplify timing; integrated digital decimation. | DC offset consumes headroom, 24-bit output is not 24-bit ENOB, multi-rail power and ESD/leakage may dominate. Compare E1 offset/CMRR and E2 DR at the actual electrode model before selecting. |
| Capacitive AC-coupled chopped IA with DC servo (E1/S1/S2 examples) | Reduces flicker and electrode offset inside measurement band, potentially microwatt circuits. | High-pass corner may erase slow ECG/EEG components; chopper input charging lowers AC impedance, ripple/servo noise and recovery must be bounded. S1/S2 are simulation; E1 is an IA, not a system. |
| Active electrode or impedance boost at contact (E3/E4 concern) | Shorter high-impedance lead may reduce cable pickup; greater effective input impedance can reduce loading. | Electronics/ESD/current near body, volume, channel power, motion/contact artifact, conductor/ground isolation and package yield need independent safety and sensor study. No safe body drive inferred. |

Literature does **not** prove the master target of 0.5–1 µV rms, >1 GΩ, >100 dB and ±300 mV simultaneously for the chosen geometry and power. A 100 dB CMRR figure under balanced source cannot stand in for real asymmetric electrode/common-mode rejection. EEG 0.1–250 Hz and ECG 0.05–500 Hz target bands differ from some cited papers; anti-alias and offset recovery must be assessed at the actual output rate. CH03's tiny-cell budget and CH02's eight-channel count must be reconciled with measured full-system power, not assumed from single-channel numbers.
